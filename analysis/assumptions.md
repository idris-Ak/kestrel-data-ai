# Value model assumptions

Every number in `value_model.py` comes either from the data or from this table. Values are (low / base / high). "Low" is the end that makes the result smaller. To change a value, edit `ASSUMPTIONS`, `GROSS_MARGIN` or `HORIZON_YEARS` at the top of the script and rerun. Sources were checked on 30 Oct 2026. Source strength: **A** = primary or independent research, **B** = industry body or large syndicated study, **C** = vendor, editorial or stated-intent survey (weak).

## From the data (not assumptions)

| Input | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Annual activity per dealer × department | 12-month total, Oct 2025–Sep 2026 | `business_monthly` (24 × 3 × 12 = 864 rows, all present) |
| Revenue per unit | Each cell's revenue ÷ activity. Group medians: Sales A$46,357/vehicle, Service A$415/job, Parts A$185/transaction | `business_monthly`. No outliers (robust z > 5: none), no month-to-month jumps |
| Closed dealer | Shepparton (VIC09) closed 2026-03-31. Its 18 zero rows (Apr–Sep, 3 depts) are explained. Its forward activity is 0, so it gets no opportunity | `dealerships.closed_date` |
| Negative rate | Bottom-2-box share of rated responses, Wilson 95% CI, n ≥ 30 to be ranked; Noosa not ranked | `kestrel/analysis_policy.sql` (D1–D5). The metrics session's `neg_rate_dealer_dept.csv` matches all 72 cells exactly |
| Dealer-to-dealer spread in true rates | Sales SD 4.7 pts, Service SD 2.8 pts, Parts SD 0 (all differences fit chance) | Empirical-Bayes method-of-moments estimate in the script |

## Assumptions

| # | Assumption | Low / base / high | Rationale and sources | Strength |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | **Representativeness:** the negative rate among all customers ÷ the rate among respondents | 0.50 / 0.75 / 1.00 | Only 1–10% of transactions leave a rating (Service 2.4%, Sales 9.4%, Parts 1.1%). Voluntary feedback over-represents extreme experiences (the "under-reporting bias" behind J-shaped rating distributions): Hu, Pavlou & Zhang (2017) *MIS Quarterly* 41(2) 449–472, https://aisel.aisnet.org/misq/vol41/iss2/8 , and CACM 2009, https://cacmb4.acm.org/magazines/2009/10/42353-overcoming-the-j-shaped-distribution-of-product-reviews . The size of the bias is a judgement; 1.0 takes responses at face value. Because it scales every cell equally, it **does not change the ranking** | A for the direction, judgement for the size |
| A2 | **P(loss):** the extra probability that a negative experience (compared with a non-negative one) loses the customer's next year of business | 0.10 / 0.20 / 0.35 (same for every department) | **Anchors.** (1) J.D. Power 2016 Australia CSI (n = 4,666): 84% of "delighted" owners say they "definitely will" revisit for paid service vs 13% of "disappointed" owners, a 71-point gap in stated intent. Source: https://www.dev.jdpower.com/?p=3218 (J.D. Power's own press-release mirror; www.jdpower.com blocks automated fetch). (2) J.D. Power 2025 US CSI: 12% of repairs were not fixed first time, and only 50% of those owners returned or planned to return. Source: https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250313701929/en . (3) Cox Automotive 2025 Fixed Ops & Ownership Study: 74% vs 44% likely to buy again from the dealer, depending on whether they returned for service. Source: https://www.coxautoinc.com/retail/resources/ownership-study/ . (4) PwC 2018 *Experience is everything*: 32% would leave a brand they like after one bad experience (stated intent, cross-industry): https://www.pwc.com/cb/en/services/consulting/pdf/customer-experience-placemat.pdf . **Discount.** Stated intent overstates behaviour. Mittal & Kamakura (2001, JMR, 100,040 auto customers) found intent and repurchase behaviour follow different curves, and for some groups rated satisfaction does not predict repurchase at all: https://doi.org/10.1509/jmkr.38.1.131.18832 . Kumar, Dalla Pozza & Ganesh (2013, *J. Retailing*) found the satisfaction–loyalty link is positive but explains little variance: https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jouret/v89y2013i3p246-262.html . So the base takes well under a third of the 71-point intent gap, and the high case sits near PwC's 32%. We found no department-specific behavioural evidence, so all departments share one value; this means it **does not change the ranking** | B (J.D. Power, Cox); C (PwC); A for the discount |
| A3 | **Service visits per customer per year** | 1.0 / 1.5 / 2.0 | Typical manufacturer intervals are 6–12 months with a 5,000–15,000 km counterpart (NRMA: https://www.mynrma.com.au/open-road/advice-and-how-to/car-maintenance/car-servicing-guide ). Australian passenger vehicles averaged 11,100–12,600 km/yr (ABS Survey of Motor Vehicle Use 2018 and 2020, final release: https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/industry/tourism-and-transport/survey-motor-vehicle-use-australia/12-months-ended-30-june-2018 ). That is about 1 scheduled service a year, plus repairs, tyres and recalls. Annual value = revenue per job × visits | B (ABS, NRMA); judgement for repairs |
| A4 | **Years between vehicle purchases** | 7 / 6 / 5 | WhichCar: Australians replace cars every 5–7 years (editorial): https://www.whichcar.com.au/advice/when-should-i-replace-my-car . BITRE average ownership of about 11 years (via Finder: https://finder.com.au/car-statistics ) counts all vehicles, not new-car buyers, so it is an upper bound and is not used. Sales annual value = revenue per vehicle ÷ cycle. This leaves out the service spend and referrals a lost buyer takes with them | C |
| A5 | **Parts purchases per customer per year** | 1 / 2 / 4 | No public source found; judgement. Parts gets zero dealer-gap value anyway, because Parts shows no real dealer differences. It matters only for the per-point lever | Judgement (weak) |
| A6 | **Gap measure** (how much of an observed gap is treated as real) | lower 95% bound / shrunk / observed | Shrinkage (empirical-Bayes beta-binomial, per department) is the standard way to avoid chasing noise in small cells. The low case counts only the part of the gap we are 97.5% sure of. The high case takes raw rates at face value | Method |
| A7 | **Target** | group median (first step) and best quartile (stretch) | Computed from shrunk rates of ranked cells in each department (open, not Noosa, n ≥ 30). Reported as two separate scenario families, not as low/high | Brief |
| A8 | **Horizon** | 1 year (headline); 3 years in sensitivity | Headline counts one year of a lost customer's spend ("annual revenue at risk"). If lost customers would otherwise have stayed about 3 years, the cumulative revenue at stake is about 3×. Cox 2025: only 51–54% of owners of vehicles under 5 years old return to the selling dealer for service (link in A2), so a long baseline tenure would overstate | B / judgement |
| A9 | **Gross margin** (indicative gross-profit view only) | Sales 4% / 8% / 11%; Service 45% / 55% / 63%; Parts 20% / 23% / 25% | Deloitte 2025 Australian Dealership Benchmarks, volume market, top-30% dealers (so optimistic for an average group): new vehicles 9–11% GP (including holdback and bonuses), service 61–63%, parts 23–25%. Source: https://www.deloitte.com/content/dam/assets-zone1/au/en/docs/industries/2025-dealership-benchmarks.pdf (we extracted the text from the PDF). NADA Data 2012/2014 (US): new vehicles 4.2%, service and parts combined about 46%: https://www.vehicleservicepros.com/aftermarket-business-world/news/article/21187913/dealer-service-departments-post-record-revenues-in-2014 , https://www.fi-magazine.com/news/nada-dealers-had-profitable-2012 . Kestrel's Service revenue probably includes parts fitted in the workshop, so the base sits below the Deloitte labour figure | B (Deloitte, NADA) |

## Known simplifications

- One negative job is treated as one customer at risk. Repeat negatives for the same customer are double-counted slightly, and we have no customer IDs to check this.
- P(loss) is incremental (negative vs non-negative). Customers who would leave anyway (for convenience, moving house or the warranty ending) are not counted.
- The Sales value counts only the annualised next vehicle. It leaves out service attachment, F&I and referrals, so it is conservative for Sales.
- Revenue at risk is **not profit**. The gross-profit view is indicative only and uses benchmark margins, not Kestrel's own.
